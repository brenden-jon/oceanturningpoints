# Style guide: design and writing

The site should read like a well-edited science publication and look like a carefully made product page.
Nothing in it should look or sound machine-generated.

## Visual design

- **Type.** The platform's system face (SF Pro on Apple devices), Inter elsewhere. One family only; no decorative serif.
  Headlines are bold with tight tracking; body text is 17–18px with generous line height.
- **Colour.** White page, near-black text, one accent blue (`--accent`) for links and controls. All other colour comes
  from imagery and scientific data scales. No decorative tints.
- **Structure without boxes.** Use space, image and type size to separate things. Avoid thin-bordered cards, coloured rules
  above blocks, and pills or badges. Where grouping is needed, use a soft grey panel with rounded corners.
- **Labels.** Sentence case. No small uppercase letter-spaced labels above headings. A short accent-coloured kicker is
  allowed above a featured headline, at most once per section.
- **Evidence labels.** Plain coloured text with a dot ("● Observed", "● Model experiment"), never a badge.
- **Controls.** Segmented controls for two to three options; plain rows for lists; pill-shaped buttons; text links end with
  "›".
- **Imagery.** Real Earth imagery (NASA Blue Marble, Black Marble) and real data maps. No stock photos, icons or illustrations
  that imitate data.
- **Motion.** Only where it carries information (data-driven animation) or a slow globe turn that stops on interaction and
  respects reduced-motion settings.

## Writing

- Plain, specific, calm. Say what is known, how it is known and how sure we are.
- Short declarative sentences. One idea per sentence where possible.
- Avoid these patterns, which read as machine-written:
  - "not X, but Y" / "X, not Y" contrasts, especially more than once on a page;
  - "the real question", "the heart of", "part of the answer", "the familiar story", "it is worth noting";
  - colons used to set up a reveal; rhetorical questions in body text;
  - groups of three adjectives or clauses for rhythm;
  - em dashes as a stylistic tic (use full stops or commas);
  - "crucial", "vital", "robust" (outside its statistical meaning), "landscape", "navigate", "unlock", "delve", "stark",
    "profound", "striking", "sheer".
- Hedge with evidence, not with adverbs: give the confidence level or the range instead of "may potentially".
- Numbers carry units and sources. Round to what the evidence supports.
- British spelling.
